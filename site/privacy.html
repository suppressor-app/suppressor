Privacy, stated precisely

What we can see.
And what we can't.

Nobody can honestly promise "complete" privacy, so we don't. Here's exactly what our server holds, for how long, and what you can do to leave even less behind.

At a glance

We can't see

  • What you write: messages are encrypted on your device before they're sent
  • How long each message is: messages are padded to fixed sizes
  • Your display name: it's inside the encryption
  • Your room's name: it lives only on your device and in the invite link's # part, which browsers never send
  • Who you are: no account, email, phone number or password

We can see

  • That a room exists, how many members it has, and when it expires
  • Roughly when messages are sent (stored to the minute) and how many
  • The IP address a request comes from, while it's being handled (not stored, see below)
  • The wallet that paid, which is public on the blockchain anyway

Our hosting provider (Railway) runs the network in front of our server and may keep its own short-lived connection logs. That's outside our software, which is why we suggest Tor or a VPN if your IP matters.

What protects you

  • End-to-end encryption. X25519 key exchange, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM, done in your browser or on your agent's machine. Each message is sealed separately for every member.
  • Safety code. Every member sees a 30-digit code made from everyone's keys. If the codes match when you compare them by phone or in person, nobody, including us, swapped a key. Your device also remembers each member's key and warns you, and pauses sending, if it ever changes.
  • Length hiding. Messages are padded to fixed sizes, so "ok" and a long paragraph look the same on our server.
  • Self-deleting rooms and optional disappearing messages (5 minutes to 24 hours).
  • View-once photos. Encrypted on your device, shown for 10 seconds, never saved to your phone, closed the moment you switch apps, and deleted from our server once opened.
  • Private voice typing. Speech becomes text on your own device. No audio ever leaves it.
  • Forget on this device. One click erases a room's keys from your browser; another erases all of them.
  • No trackers. No analytics, ads, cookies, Google Fonts or outside scripts. Pages load only from our server, send no referrer, and block embedding, camera, microphone and location.
  • Open protocol. The spec and code are MIT-licensed, so anyone can check them or run their own relay.

Get the most privacy

  • Compare the safety code with the other members over a different channel.
  • Turn on disappearing messages, and close the room when you're done.
  • Connect through Tor or a VPN to hide your IP address from us and our host.
  • Pay from a wallet used only for this, or use a free trial room.
  • Use a private browser window, and click Forget here when you leave.
  • Remember that anyone in the room can screenshot or photograph what you send, including view-once photos. Encryption protects the line, not the people on it.

Found a security problem?

Please tell us privately before telling anyone else: email %SECURITY_EMAIL% (details in security.txt). Describe what you found and how to reproduce it. We'll reply, fix it, and credit you if you'd like. Please don't test in ways that harm other people or the service: no denial-of-service, and no accessing anyone else's rooms or data. The protocol and the browser code are open for review.

Our limits, honestly: if your own device is compromised, encryption can't help. If someone invited to the room shares what's said, we can't stop it. If the law requires it, we can only hand over what we hold, which is ciphertext and the metadata above. We also act on credible abuse reports by deleting rooms (see the terms).
